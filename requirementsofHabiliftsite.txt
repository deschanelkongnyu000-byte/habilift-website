# HabiLift Website Redesign and Development Blueprint

## 1. Project Vision

### HabiLift: A Human First Mental Healthcare Ecosystem

The new HabiLift website should communicate one central idea:

> **Mental healthcare should feel connected, human, safe, and accessible.**

The website should not feel like a technology company trying to sell a mental health product.

It should feel like a **trusted place where people can understand their options, feel seen, discover support, and understand how HabiLift connects the people and institutions involved in care.**

Technology should support the story rather than become the story.

The website should therefore combine:

* Human emotion
* Trust
* Safety
* Hope
* Professional credibility
* Accessibility
* Strong visual storytelling
* Clear explanations
* Realistic use cases
* Strong ecosystem positioning
* Institutional credibility
* Conversion paths
* Editorial content
* Custom content management

---

# 2. Problems With the Current Website

The current website contains many good concepts, but several elements compete for attention.

### 2.1 Too much information too early

The homepage introduces:

* individuals
* professionals
* hospitals
* schools
* companies
* NGOs
* government
* correctional facilities
* families
* AI
* security
* teletherapy
* referrals
* ecosystem
* dashboards
* statistics

This makes HabiLift appear very broad before the visitor has emotionally connected with the central problem.

### 2.2 The human story is too far down

Mental healthcare is highly emotional.

The visitor should encounter a feeling of:

> “This understands what I or someone I care about may be going through.”

before encountering large ecosystem statistics.

### 2.3 The website looks more like a technology platform

Terms such as:

> “command centre”

> “live capacity”

> “outcome monitoring”

> “AI pre-assessment”

> “connected ecosystem”

are useful, but too much of this language can make the product feel institutional and technical.

HabiLift needs to show the **human outcome behind the technology.**

### 2.4 The ecosystem is explained conceptually rather than visually

The current ecosystem section tells users that everything is connected.

The new site should **show them what that connection looks like.**

For example:

**Person → Habi → Specialist → Consultation → Care Plan → Referral → Hospital / Pharmacy → Continued Support**

This should become an interactive visual story rather than simply a list of stakeholder categories.

### 2.5 The site needs stronger differentiation between audiences

A person looking for help should not have to mentally process the same information as:

* a psychologist
* a hospital
* an NGO
* a company
* a school
* a government institution

The website should allow each visitor to quickly identify:

> **“This is what HabiLift does for someone like me.”**

# 4. Homepage Structure

The homepage should follow a carefully designed emotional progression.

## Section 01 — Hero

Do not begin with:

> “One connected ecosystem. One shared mission.”

Instead, begin with the human problem.

Possible direction:

### Mental healthcare should never feel like something you have to navigate alone.

Supporting text:

> HabiLift connects people with trusted mental health professionals and brings the people and organizations involved in care closer together.

Primary action:

**Find Support**

Secondary action:

**See How HabiLift Works**

### Visual Direction

The hero should be highly visual.

Rather than a generic dashboard screenshot, show a human centered scene or carefully art directed illustration representing connection, support and calm.

The HabiLift visual identity should be immediately recognizable.

---

# 5. Emotional Connection Section

Immediately after the hero, introduce relatable situations.

## “Sometimes, asking for help is the hardest part.”

Use a horizontal or card based storytelling experience.

Examples:

### “I don't know what I'm feeling.”

Habi helps you begin with a private, guided conversation.

### “I know I need someone to talk to.”

Find a verified professional who fits your needs.

### “I'm supporting someone I care about.”

Understand how to support them and where professional help fits.

### “Care shouldn't stop after one conversation.”

HabiLift helps create continuity around ongoing care.

This section creates emotional recognition before introducing technical features.

---

# 6. Meet Habi

Habi should become a recognizable character and product experience rather than simply an AI feature.

## Meet Habi

### A gentle first step toward understanding what you need.

Show an actual conversation interface.

The visitor should see a short realistic interaction.

For example:

> “You don't have to have the right words. We can start with how you've been feeling lately.”

Then show a few conversation stages.

### Important positioning

Habi should consistently be described as:

**A care companion that supports the beginning of the journey.**

Never position Habi as:

* a therapist
* a doctor
* a replacement for professionals
* an autonomous diagnostic system

The human professional should remain central.

---

# 7. The HabiLift Journey

This should become one of the most visually important sections on the website.

## From “I need help” to “I'm not alone.”

Create a large interactive journey:

### 01

**Start privately**

Tell Habi what you are experiencing.

↓

### 02

**Understand what you need**

A structured pre assessment helps organize what you share.

↓

### 03

**Meet the right professional**

Discover or match with a verified specialist.

↓

### 04

**Talk securely**

Access consultation through app or browser.

↓

### 05

**Build your care journey**

Continue with appointments, communication, progress and care planning.

↓

### 06

**Connect when more support is needed**

Referrals and coordinated support can involve other professionals and institutions.

The visual should make the journey understandable without requiring the visitor to read paragraphs.

# 11. Visual Storytelling Strategy

The new website should use visuals deliberately.

Not every section needs an image.

Instead, every visual should answer one of three questions:

### Does this help me understand?

Use:

* diagrams
* product interfaces
* journey maps
* ecosystem visualizations

### Does this help me feel?

Use:

* human photography
* carefully directed lifestyle photography
* empathetic illustrations
* subtle motion

### Does this help me trust?

Use:

* specialist profiles
* verification indicators
* security explanations
* institutional imagery
* real product interfaces
* founder/team imagery
* partnerships
* credentials

---

* Human
* Calm
* Warm

# 13. Photography Direction

# 22. Custom CMS

The CMS should be designed specifically for HabiLift rather than trying to imitate WordPress.

The public website should be treated as a **content platform**, while the HabiLift application remains the actual care platform.

The CMS should allow authorized administrators to manage website content without developer intervention.

## CMS Dashboard

### Dashboard

Show:

* Published content
* Drafts
* Pending review
* Recent changes
* Scheduled publications
* Website activity

---

# 24. CMS Page Builder

Do not build a completely unrestricted drag and drop page builder.

Instead, use a **structured section builder**.

For example:

**Page**

→ Hero

→ Introduction

→ Feature section

→ Image story

→ Journey

→ Testimonials

→ CTA

Each section has controlled fields.

This keeps the website visually consistent while still giving administrators substantial control.

---

# 25. CMS Localization

Since HabiLift supports EN/FR, multilingual content should be designed into the CMS from the beginning.

Each content item should support:

**English**

and

**French**

with clear translation status.

Administrators should be able to see:

* English published
* French draft
* Translation missing
* Both published

Do not treat translation as an afterthought.

---

# 29. Motion Design

Animation should communicate, not decorate.

Use motion for:

### Connection

Nodes gradually connect.

### Journey

A user moves through the care pathway.

### Habi

Subtle conversational animation.

### Ecosystem

Stakeholders respond to selection.

### Statistics

Numbers can reveal themselves as the visitor reaches them.

### Page transitions

Subtle transitions can make navigation feel cohesive.

Avoid excessive:

* bouncing
* floating
* parallax
* spinning
* particle effects

Mental healthcare should feel calm.

---

# 30. Responsive Design

The mobile experience should not simply be a collapsed desktop website.

Design separately for:

### Mobile

Primary priority.

### Tablet

Intermediate layout.

### Desktop

Large visual storytelling.

The website should remain usable under:

* slow connections
* smaller screens
* accessibility settings
* reduced motion preferences

---

# 31. Accessibility

Accessibility should be part of the architecture.

Include:

* semantic HTML
* keyboard navigation
* proper contrast
* alt text
* accessible forms
* screen reader support
* focus states
* reduced motion
* readable typography
* meaningful headings
* accessible video controls

Mental healthcare websites especially need to avoid creating unnecessary cognitive load.

---

# 32. Performance

The visual richness must not produce a slow website.

Use:

* optimized images
* responsive image sizes
* modern image formats
* lazy loading
* optimized fonts
* code splitting
* minimal JavaScript where unnecessary
* compressed video
* CDN delivery
* caching

The design should be visually rich **without becoming heavy.**

---

# 33. Technical Architecture

The website should be developed as a modern custom application.

### Frontend

A modern component based framework.

### Backend

A dedicated CMS/API backend.

### Database

Structured relational database.

### Media

Dedicated media storage and optimization.

### Authentication

Secure administrator authentication.

### API

Structured API between CMS and public website.

### Deployment

Separate environments:

**Development**

→ **Staging**

→ **Production**

No WordPress.

The website CMS and the HabiLift care application should also have clearly separated responsibilities.

---

Responsible for:

* Appointments
* Consultations

### CMS

Responsible for:

* Website content
* Articles
* Stories

# 43. Final Product Direction

The new HabiLift website should feel like a combination of:

**A trusted mental health organization**

*

**A modern healthcare platform**

*

**A human centered digital product**

*

**A credible technology company**

*

**A mental health education resource**

It should not feel like a conventional SaaS landing page.

The strongest visual and structural principle should be:

> **People first. Care second. Technology in service of both.**

The website should make visitors understand that HabiLift is not simply an app for booking a therapist.

It is building a connected environment around mental healthcare, while keeping the individual and the human professional at the center.

The redesign should not be:

**“Let's make the current HabiLift website look better.”**

It should be:

**“Let's design the digital front door to an entire mental healthcare ecosystem.”**

That difference should guide every design and development decision.
